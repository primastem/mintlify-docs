## Introduction

PrimaSTEM consists of a programmable robot, a control board and a set of command tokens. You can use it to prevent and correct cognitive impairments in elderly people.

### Who it is for

- Elderly people with mild cognitive impairments
- People who have had neurological diseases
- Patients in early stages of neurodegenerative diseases

### Contraindications

- Severe vision impairments
- Significant motor impairments of the arms
- Severe dementia
- Acute psychiatric disorders

## First session

It takes about 35 minutes. Read the contraindications above before you start. If you don't have the device yet, you can do every step in the [simulator](https://simulator.primastem.com).

<Steps>
  <Step title="Getting to know the kit, 10 minutes">
    Show the robot, the control board and the “Forward”, “Right” and “Left” tokens. The participant places one “Forward” token on the control board, presses **▶ Start** and watches the robot move. [Quick start](/en/quickstart) explains how the control board and the robot work.
  </Step>
  <Step title="First route, 10 minutes">
    Mark a goal two or three cells away from the robot. The participant lays out the tokens, runs the program and checks whether the robot got there. In the simulator, use the [easy maze](https://simulator.primastem.com/#s=l1144402&r=75-525-0&m=2101).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Break, 5 minutes">
    Rest after 15–20 minutes of work.
  </Step>
  <Step title="A shape, 10 minutes">
    The participant builds a program that makes the robot trace a square. In the simulator, open the [square task](https://simulator.primastem.com/#sh=square): the robot has to follow the dashed outline.
  </Step>
</Steps>

At the end, ask what felt easy and what was hard. The answer tells you where to start next time.

## General principles

With age and neurodegenerative diseases, memory can get worse. Abstract thinking, learning and creativity can decline. Classes with the PrimaSTEM kit train different cognitive functions in a game format. This helps slow these processes down.

### How to organize classes

- One session lasts 30–45 minutes.
- Classes take place 2–3 times a week.
- Work alone or in small groups of 2–4 people.
- After every 15–20 minutes of work, take a break of 5–10 minutes.

### How to run classes

1. Increase the difficulty of tasks gradually.
2. Take each participant's characteristics and limits into account.
3. Create a friendly atmosphere.
4. Alternate different types of exercises.
5. Always tell the participant what went well, and praise them.

## What the classes train

### Cognitive functions

- Intellectual activity
- Spatial thinking and memory
- Visual and auditory-verbal memory
- Logical thinking and mental arithmetic
- Planning and control of one's actions
- Peripheral vision

### Communication and mood

- The classes support personal potential and social activity.
- The participant can express themselves.
- It is easier for the participant to talk with others.
- The classes lift the mood.

## Training and games

### 1. First exercises (getting used to the kit)

- Get to know the command tokens.
- Move the robot in a straight line.
- Do simple turns.
- Build a short sequence of commands.

**Materials**

- [Quick start](/en/quickstart)
- [Book 1](/en/book-1/book-1), Activity 2

### 2. Spatial thinking training

#### Route

- Find the shortest path.
- Go around obstacles.
- Build a route from a random set of command tokens.
- Follow a given algorithm.
- Choose the correct route from several options.
- Build a reverse route.

#### Difficulty levels

1. Find the endpoint from a given route.
2. Choose the correct route from a list.
3. Find the starting point from a known endpoint.

#### Familiar rooms

- Build a model of a familiar space: rooms or hallways of a house.
- Plan a route through a room.
- Navigate a maze.

**Materials**

- Mazes in the simulator: [easy](https://simulator.primastem.com/#s=l1144402&r=75-525-0&m=2101), [medium](https://simulator.primastem.com/#s=l1144502&r=75-675-0&m=400113232131), [hard](https://simulator.primastem.com/#s=l1144602&r=75-825-0&m=0014243444540212223242). The robot starts in a corner, the flag marks the goal.
- [Appendix 1. Moving on the map](/en/book-1/attachment-1)
- [Appendix 2. Mission cards](/en/book-1/attachment-2)
- [Book 1](/en/book-1/book-1), Activity 4: predict where the robot will stop.
- [Appendix 3. Draw the path for a program](/en/book-1/attachment-3)

### 3. Planning training

- Build an algorithm for everyday tasks:
  - “I'm at the store: buy from a list”
  - “I'm getting ready to go outside: what do I need to do”
- Work with cards that show objects and actions.

**Materials**

- [Appendix 2. Mission cards](/en/book-1/attachment-2)
- [Appendix 4. Find and fix an error in the program](/en/book-1/attachment-4)

### 4. Spatial memory training

- Memorize a route and repeat it. You can show the route or say it aloud.
- Use mnemonic techniques, for example the Cicero method.
- Play “MEMO”: find and remember pairs of cards.

**Materials**

- [Stairs in the simulator](https://simulator.primastem.com/#top=cfun.r004&fn=cfor,crig,cfor,clef&s=l1144502&r=75-675-0&run): watch the robot drive, close the window and lay out the route on the control board from memory.

### 5. Mathematical games

- Math maze: the robot moves across cells with problems.
- Arithmetic route: at each step, add or subtract the same number.

**Materials**

- [Teacher's guide](/en/teachersguide), section “8. Arithmetic”.
- [Drawing examples](/en/mathdrawings), group “Spirals”: each step grows by the same number.

### 6. Word games

- Find proverbs on the field.
- Compose words from scattered letters.
- Build a route from pictures with specific sounds.

**Materials**

- [Audio files reference](/en/sounds_reference): the control board voices tokens, letters and place names in 21 languages.

### 7. Pencil games (drawing)

#### Graphic dictations

- Build an algorithm from a drawing.
- Complete the symmetrical half.
- Find and fix an error in the algorithm.
- Draw a mirror reflection.
- Rotate a figure by 90°.
- Create patterns and ornaments with loops.

**Materials**

- Trace a shape along its outline in the simulator: [square](https://simulator.primastem.com/#sh=square), [triangle](https://simulator.primastem.com/#sh=triangle), [house](https://simulator.primastem.com/#sh=house), [star](https://simulator.primastem.com/#sh=star).
- [Drawing examples](/en/mathdrawings): each program sits next to its finished drawing.

### 8. Communication games

#### Competitions

- Collect as many bonus tokens as possible
- Game like “Monopoly” with the robot
- Word game with letter search
- “Labyrinth” board game for two players

**Materials**

- Puzzles for pairs and groups are collected below, in the “Puzzles” section.

## How to assess the result

### What to track

- Test cognitive functions regularly.
- Watch how task performance changes from session to session.
- Ask participants what they liked and what they did not.
- Keep a session diary.

### Signs of success

- The participant performs tasks better.
- The participant manages harder exercises.
- The participant rates the sessions positively.
- The participant uses the skills in daily life.

## How to adapt the program

### For each participant

- Take physical limitations into account.
- Adjust the pace of sessions.
- Change tasks to match the participant's interests.
- Make an individual plan for raising the difficulty step by step.

### Group work

- Form groups by ability level.
- Add competition to the sessions.
- Encourage mutual help.
- Create a comfortable atmosphere for communication.
