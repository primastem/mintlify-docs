## INTRODUCTION

PrimaStem is a robotic complex consisting of a programmable robot and a control panel with a set of command tokens. The complex can be used for prevention and correction of cognitive impairments in elderly people.

### Target Audience

- Elderly people with mild cognitive impairments
- Persons after neurological diseases
- Patients in early stages of neurodegenerative diseases

### Contraindications

- Severe vision impairments
- Pronounced motor impairments of upper limbs
- Severe dementia
- Acute mental disorders

## GENERAL PRINCIPLES

With age and in neurodegenerative diseases, memory deterioration, reduced ability for abstract thinking, learning, and creative development may occur. Classes with the PrimaStem kit help slow down these processes by training various cognitive functions in a game form.

### Methodological Recommendations

### Organization of Classes

- Duration of one session: 30-45 minutes
- Frequency of classes: 2-3 times a week
- Format: individual or small groups (2-4 people)
- Required breaks: 5-10 minutes after every 15-20 minutes of work

### Principles of Conducting Classes

1. Gradual increase in task complexity
2. Consideration of individual characteristics and limitations
3. Creating a positive emotional atmosphere
4. Alternating different types of exercises
5. Mandatory feedback and encouragement

## MAIN AREAS OF CORRECTION

### Cognitive Functions

- Stimulation of intellectual activity
- Development of spatial thinking and memory
- Improvement of visual and auditory-verbal memory
- Training of logical thinking and mental arithmetic
- Development of planning and activity control skills
- Training of peripheral vision

### Socio-Emotional Aspects

- Maintaining personal potential and social activity
- Creating opportunities for self-expression
- Overcoming communication barriers
- Forming a positive emotional background

## TRAINING OPTIONS AND GAME EXERCISES

### 1. Basic Exercises (Adaptation Period)

- Familiarization with command tokens
- Simple robot movements in a straight line
- Performing basic turns
- Creating simple command sequences

### 2. Spatial Thinking Training

#### Route Building

- Shortest path
- With obstacles
- Using a random set of command tokens
- According to a given algorithm
- Choosing the correct route from proposed options
- Building a return route

#### Difficulty Levels

1. Determining the end point by a given route
2. Choosing the correct route from a list
3. Determining the starting point by a known end point

#### Working with Real Spaces

- Modeling familiar premises (rooms, corridors of the house)
- Planning routes indoors
- Passing through mazes

### 3. Planning Skills Training

- Building an algorithm of everyday actions:
    - "I'm in a store – buy according to the list"
    - "I'm getting ready to go outside – what are my actions?"
- Using cards with images of objects and actions

### 4. Spatial Memory Training

- Memorizing and reproducing routes (visually, by ear)
- Using mnemonics (Cicero Method)
- Game type "MEMO":
    - Finding and memorizing paired cards

### 5. Mathematical Games

- Mathematical mazes – moving through cells with examples
- Arithmetic route – adding/subtracting the same number

### 6. Word Games

- Finding proverbs on the field
- Composing words from scattered letters
- Creating routes by images with certain sounds

### 7. Pencil Games (Drawing)

#### Graphic Dictations

- Building an algorithm based on a drawing
- Completing a symmetrical half
- Correcting errors in the algorithm
- Drawing a mirror image
- Rotating a figure by 90 degrees
- Creating patterns and ornaments using loops

### 8. Communicative Games

#### Competitive Exercises

- Collecting the maximum number of bonus tokens
- "Monopoly" type game with a robot
- Word game with letter search
- Board game "Labyrinth" for two players

## EFFECTIVENESS EVALUATION

### Control Methods

- Regular testing of cognitive functions
- Observation of task performance dynamics
- Collecting feedback from participants
- Keeping a session diary

### Success Criteria

- Improvement in task performance indicators
- Increasing complexity of performed exercises
- Positive subjective evaluation by participants
- Transfer of skills to everyday life

## RECOMMENDATIONS FOR PROGRAM ADAPTATION

### Individualization

- Consideration of physical limitations
- Adaptation of class pace
- Modification of tasks according to participant's interests
- Creating an individual progression plan

### Group Work

- Forming groups by ability level
- Organizing competitive elements
- Supporting mutual assistance
- Creating a comfortable social environment
